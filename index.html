Publications

Air pollution-related health impacts from domestic waste burning and associated interventions: the merits of a traditional versus machine learning scoping review methodology.
Spinger: Environmental Monitoring and Assessment (2026)
Tested whether machine learning can partially automate a scoping review of air pollution health impacts from domestic waste burning, finding that while the ML approach could handle initial searches and screening (with a tendency toward false positives rather than the more problematic false negatives), human verification remained necessary. Thus, recommend a hybrid human-ML approach. Our review identified diverse health impacts of waste burning, including low birth weight, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, respiratory problems like asthma and wheeze, cancer risk, and mortality, while noting that few studies proposed or evaluated interventions.

Explaining Play-style Behaviours with Hierarchical Policies and Shapley Values
IN PROGRESS UNDER REVIEW (AIIDE 2026)
We present a framework for interpreting artificial player strategies using explainable reinforcement learning. Our approach learns a hierarchical policy, where a high-level controller selects among low-level policies, each representing a distinct play-style. We apply explainability methods to analyse both the choice of play-style and the agent's resulting in-game behaviour. In particular, we utilise shapley values for these explanations. However, since traditionally shapley values assume feature independence, we use groupShapley to provide more reliable and interpretable explanations in structured environments. We evaluate our method in MiniDungeons and Settlers of Catan, a high-dimensional and strategically rich environment. Our results show that groupShapley Values yield clearer and more consistent interpretations of play-style behaviours, offering insights that may support game understanding and strategic exploration for players and designers alike.
